Short Fiction

And None Could Go

By EMToast Staff
October 16, 2026

MARTIN

Susan had her purse and one hand on the doorknob. I had one shoe on. David said goodbye meant God be with ye. A blessing shortened by centuries until nobody knew what they were asking for anymore. I thought of all the times I had said it, to strangers, to friends, to people I never wanted to see again. God be with ye. And now I couldn't leave.

“It's just a word,” Susan said.

That was easy for her to say. She was still standing there.

SUSAN

I wanted to go home. That was all. I had been ready to go home for twenty minutes, and now I was being held hostage by the history of the English language.

“See you later,” I said.

“What if you don't want to see them later?” Martin asked.

I looked at him. He had one shoe on.

“I'm leaving,” I said, and nobody answered.

DAVID

Words outlive their meanings. That's their peculiar talent. Goodbye, once a blessing, becomes a habit, a noise at the end of a conversation, something you say while looking for your keys. Then somebody tells you what it meant, and suddenly the habit becomes a question.

I ate my cereal. The milk had gone warm.

RAY

I came in with groceries. They were all sitting around, not talking, like somebody had died.

“What's going on?”

“Goodbye,” Martin said.

“Ah,” I told him. “That's why I never say it.”

I say I'm outta here. You announce the action, you perform the action. No blessing, no theological entanglements. David said I was invoking the concept of being outside. I told him he was thinking too much.

Then I left.

MARTIN

Ray came back five minutes later to tell us that farewell might also be a problem. David threw a spoon at him. Ray left again.

I tried I'm outta here. I got as far as the hallway before wondering whether I was still here.

Susan went home without saying anything. David finished his cereal.

“I'm never leaving this apartment,” I said.

“You said that last week,” David replied.

“And I'm still here.”

DAVID

At midnight, Martin said good night. We looked at him. He looked at us.

“Oh, for God's sake,” he said.

Nobody moved.
